title: Building an Emergency Supply Kit and Go-Bag
source_name: Ready.gov (FEMA) – team summary
source_url: https://www.ready.gov/kit
category: general
---
An emergency kit is a collection of basic items your household may need during and after a disaster. Keep a larger kit at home and a smaller go-bag you can grab quickly when evacuating.

Basic supplies: water (about one gallon per person per day for several days), non-perishable food and a manual can opener, a flashlight, a battery-powered or hand-crank radio, extra batteries, a first aid kit, a whistle to signal for help, dust masks, moist towelettes, garbage bags, a wrench or pliers to turn off utilities, and local maps.

Personal needs: a supply of prescription medications, glasses or contact lenses, infant formula and diapers, pet food and extra water for pets, and items for anyone with disabilities or access needs, such as spare hearing aid batteries or mobility aids.

Documents and money: copies of identification, insurance policies, bank account records and medical information, stored in a waterproof bag or saved digitally in a secure place. Carry some cash because ATMs and card readers may not work during power outages.

Also pack phone chargers and a backup battery, a change of clothes, sturdy shoes, a sleeping bag or warm blanket, and hygiene items. Check your kit every six months: replace expired food, water, medicine and batteries, and update it as your family's needs change.
