title: Hurricane Evacuation Preparedness
source_name: Ready.gov (FEMA) – team summary

Hurricanes bring dangerous winds, heavy rain, storm surge and inland flooding. Storm surge and flooding cause most hurricane deaths, so evacuation decisions matter.

Know your zone before the season starts. Many coastal counties publish evacuation zones; look up your zone on your local emergency management website and learn the routes out of your area. Plan where you will go: a friend or relative outside the zone, a hotel inland, or a public shelter. Check in advance whether shelters accept pets.

Understand the alerts. A hurricane watch means hurricane conditions are possible in the area, usually within about 48 hours. A hurricane warning means hurricane conditions are expected, usually within about 36 hours. When local officials issue an evacuation order, leave. Do not wait for the storm to arrive; roads can flood and become blocked before the strongest winds.

Before you leave: fill your car's gas tank early, charge phones and power banks, bring your go-bag, medications and important documents, unplug appliances, and turn off utilities if officials tell you to. Let someone outside the area know where you are going.

If you cannot evacuate or are told to shelter in place, go to a small interior room, closet or hallway on the lowest floor that is not likely to flood. Stay away from windows. Never drive or walk through flood water: just six inches of moving water can knock a person down and one foot can sweep a vehicle away.

After the storm, return only when officials say it is safe. Watch for downed power lines, damaged structures and contaminated flood water. Never run a generator indoors or in a garage because of carbon monoxide poisoning.
