title: Flash Flood Safety
source_name: National Weather Service – team summary
source_url: https://www.weather.gov/safety/flood
category: flood
---
Flash floods can develop within minutes to a few hours of heavy rain, especially in hilly terrain, near rivers and creeks, in low-water crossings and in urban areas with lots of pavement. They can happen at night when people are asleep and cannot see rising water.

Know the alerts. A flood watch means conditions are favorable for flooding; be prepared to move. A flood warning or flash flood warning means flooding is happening or about to happen; move to higher ground immediately. A flash flood emergency is issued for severe threats to life.

Turn around, don't drown. Never drive, walk or swim through flood water. Water may be deeper than it looks, roads beneath it may be washed out, and moving water is extremely powerful. Most flood deaths happen in vehicles. If your car is caught in rising water and the water is not moving, get out and move to higher ground. If water is rising inside the vehicle, get onto the roof.

If you are camping or live near a creek or river, know how you would get to higher ground quickly and keep a way to receive alerts, such as a weather radio or phone alerts, especially overnight.

After a flood, avoid flood water: it can contain sewage, chemicals, debris and live electrical wires. Do not return home until officials say it is safe. Photograph damage before cleanup for insurance and assistance claims, and throw away food that came into contact with flood water.
